Sustaining Voice: Digital Media, Indigenous Representation, and Institutional Trust
Thursday October 22, 2026 10:15am - 10:35am PDT
501 Chiwawa
You're checked in!
Museums have produced video and media for decades—but what does it mean to design media that sustains voice over time?


In an era shaped by AI-generated content, rapid platform change, and evolving expectations around access, the question is no longer only how voice is captured. It is also how voice is mediated, translated, maintained, and trusted. This session examines how digital systems and human processes shape how museums represent the voices of artists, people, and communities.


While many institutions create subject-based videos, these projects are often treated as one-off assets tied to a specific exhibition or platform. This case study reframes media as infrastructure: a network of decisions across production, interviewing, editing, captioning, interface design, playback, and maintenance. Together, these decisions determine how voice is experienced, accessed, and understood.


Drawing from Crystal Bridges Museum of American Art’s Artist Voices video series, developed as part of the museum’s 2026 expansion reinstallation, this session explores how museums can design media systems that preserve the integrity of voice across in-gallery video stations, web platforms, social media, and future reuse. The series focuses on living artists, where voice is collaborative but shaped through many stages of production. Interviewing, editing, pacing, translation, captioning, and platform design all influence what is shared, emphasized, and sustained.


The session will give particular attention to the representation of Indigenous voices within artist-centered media, including mini-documentaries featuring artists Nicholas Galanin and Lily Hope. In this context, sustaining voice requires more than recording an interview or presenting an artist’s words. It requires attention to agency, authorship, cultural specificity, and how institutional editing can either preserve complexity or unintentionally flatten it. The framing of questions, filming relationships, and edit decisions all shape how Indigenous perspectives are carried forward.


A video may begin as a conversation, but it reaches audiences through layers of editing, translation, accessibility, design, and institutional decision-making. These choices shape meaning, access, and trust.


The session will also address practical decisions that support sustainability, including dual-language open captions, standardized media formats, multi-platform design, consistent playback, and coordination between content, design, technology, and installation teams. These choices show how accessibility, consistency, and technical planning are essential components of ethical digital interpretation.


Attendees will leave with strategies for designing digital media as durable systems rather than isolated outputs, with approaches for balancing authenticity, accessibility, longevity, and audience trust.
