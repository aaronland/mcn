Collections Online in 3.5 Months: An Honest Look
Thursday October 22, 2026 2:15pm - 3:00pm PDT
512 Willapa
You're checked in!
In early 2026, LACMA shipped Collections Online — a public search and discovery site for the museum's collection — in roughly 3.5 months from kickoff to launch. The team was small (LACMA's digital team of two) and the work was deeply collaborative across overlapping skills: LACMA's Senior Director of Publishing — portfolio across editorial, design, and web/digital — anchoring the experience perspective and the internal advocacy that kept the work aligned across the organization; an independent backend engineer on the data platform application; the Folder Design team behind the visual design system and reference demos; the Urban Insight team behind the Netlify-hosted Next.js front end — search integration, frontend architecture, performance, and the discovery experience; and LACMA's Director of Digital Strategy & Infrastructure, who architected the overall environment and built the systems around the data platform (image, AI, search, monitoring, admin tooling, data flows), iterated on intent, and held day-to-day validation, troubleshooting, and coordination.

At the center sits a custom data platform — a FastAPI on ECS application backed by PostgreSQL/RDS — that ingests eleven TMS SQL views (about 1.1 million rows per cycle) plus 119,000 AI-enriched records from DynamoDB, applies user-defined transformation rulesets, and publishes through REST, GraphQL, and a bulk OpenSearch index. Two VPCs joined by peering, schema/rules admin UI, 6-hour ingest cadence with admin-triggered refresh.

A Lambda + SQS image processing pipeline moves 68,000 image-bearing objects (and derivatives) into KMS-encrypted S3. An AI enrichment layer — a SageMaker-hosted SigLIP 2 vision model run ephemerally on g5.xlarge, plus a long-running EC2 service for visual descriptions, embeddings, and search-time text encoding — produces the metadata for hybrid vector search and accessibility. An OpenSearch cluster handles faceted keyword search. A pair of admin tools — diagnostic Lookups and editorial Overrides — keep curatorial, engineering, and the data pipeline honest about what is surfacing on the site.

On the frontend, Next.js + Netlify deliver the public experience, CDN-optimized for scale.

- End-to-End Architecture: the AWS/FastAPI stack, frontend delivery, and operations — with cost and performance numbers for each piece.

- The Reality of AI-Assisted Engineering: where Claude and Gemini delivered 10x acceleration, where they introduced subtle costly bugs, and where we reverted to traditional engineering.

- Operationalizing AI: prompt engineering, agent-based workflows, and the scaffolding that makes AI-assisted work repeatable across a hybrid team.
